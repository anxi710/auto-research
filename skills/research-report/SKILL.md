# Research Report

Improve the researcher's understanding of the argument and the decision it supports.
Let the host workflow govern process, evidence records, versions, and permission.
Let document tools and visual assets govern rendering.

## Establish the writing contract

Read the request, supplied scientific text, available evidence, and existing decisions.
Identify the reader, intended use or decision, report type, editing scope, and output destination.
Infer routine presentation choices from that context.
Ask only for missing information that changes the argument or permitted action.
Keep a short question at its requested scale.
Do not expand a writing task into new research, data analysis, or evidence collection.

Use [argument design](references/argument-design.md) to select a reading path for the report type.
Use [reader review](references/reader-review.md) to check comprehension, evidence bindings, and decision requests.
Read [design sources](references/design-sources.md) when assessing the limited basis for these writing choices.

## Build an argument from the evidence

Organize around the research question and the claims needed to answer it.
Compare approaches or explanations under shared conditions instead of listing papers.
Define the concepts needed to understand a distinction or causal chain.
Explain which links are observed and which are inferred.
State the answer at the strength and scope the evidence supports.
Address material counterevidence and competing explanations.
Explain what missing observation could change the judgment.
Distinguish source findings, observed results, author inference, recommendations, and untested proposals.
Keep actual reading depth visible where it limits a claim.
Never turn an abstract, excerpt, or search snippet into an implied full-paper reading.
Mark a missing source location or unresolved support rather than inventing it.

## Provide two levels of reading

For a substantial report, provide a concise summary and a self-contained full account.
Lead the summary with the question, answer, main basis, limits, and decision needed.
Write the full account so a researcher can understand it without the summary or a technical index.
Include the context, comparisons, reasoning, evidence limits, and unresolved alternatives needed for that understanding.
Derive the summary from the finished account.
Preserve the same conditions, uncertainty, negative findings, and scientific meaning in both.
Follow a required venue structure while retaining these functions.
Use meaningful section names and explicit transitions between evidence and judgment.
Keep claim IDs in traceability links rather than using them as the narrative.
Make the main argument understandable without reading JSON or operational records.

## Preserve authority and scientific identity

Bind every delivered view to one scientific version and the same evidence versions.
Identify exact revisions or digests in an existing index, or a short source note when no index exists.
Reuse existing evidence locations and claim IDs when available.
Keep earlier presented versions available when scientific content changes.
Check the views for scientific agreement rather than assuming a byte digest proves it.

Proceed with expression, organization, and reading-path edits within the authorized scope.
Treat changes to scientific facts, interpretation, claim strength, or evidence versions as scientific revisions.
Follow the host workflow's existing human-review boundary for those revisions and dependent work.
Do not use unapproved AI scientific critique to perform dependent revisions without matching human approval or a scoped waiver.
If no host workflow is present, use the current request's authority without inventing stages, schemas, or approval files.
Present an unresolved scientific choice to the researcher before work that depends on it.
Do not describe a delivered draft or AI assessment as human approval.

## Finish with a reader check

Read as a researcher outside the project who has no access to the technical records.
Check that this reader can restate the question, judgment, reasons, limits, evidence locations, and pending decision.
Repair an unclear reasoning step within scope or expose the scientific gap for review.
Give each review question a location, concrete options, and the consequence of each option.
Point to the exact text and evidence needed to decide.
Report unresolved support and reading limits with the deliverable.
Choose a figure or table only when it answers a reader's question more clearly than the prose.
Give its comparison, take-away, and support limits in a readable caption or nearby explanation.
Check headings, citations, evidence links, and the path from the summary to the fuller account at the final delivery location.
Let the argument determine the visual choice, rather than filling a template's available slots.
Treat formatting, LaTeX compilation, and hashes as technical checks rather than writing acceptance.
