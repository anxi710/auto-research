# Design the argument for its use

Choose the report's purpose from the reader's question and the decision they need to make.
Use the paths below as reasoning guides rather than mandatory section templates.
Keep supplied conclusions provisional when their support is unresolved.

| Report type | Reader's central question | Build the account around |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thematic review | What is known, why do findings differ, and which explanation is useful here? | A question, relevant concepts, comparable approaches, agreements and disagreements, and the boundary of the synthesis. |
| Experimental design | Which observation would distinguish the live explanations? | The uncertainty, competing predictions, a discriminating comparison, observable outcomes, confounds, and the decision each outcome would support. |
| Result analysis | What did the evidence show, and what judgment does it permit? | Actual observations, comparison conditions, variation, negative results, competing interpretations, and the remaining uncertainty. |
| Progress report | What changed in our understanding, and what choice follows? | The previous state, new evidence or completed work, its consequence for the argument, unresolved work, and the next decision. |

## Make comparisons explain something

Select comparison axes that could change the answer.
For a review, these may include assumptions, tasks, evidence type, mechanism, or evaluation conditions.
For a design, state how the same observation would bear differently on the competing hypotheses.
For results, separate a measured difference from an explanation of that difference.
For progress, distinguish completed activity from a change in scientific understanding.
Do not make incomparable conditions look like a shared benchmark.
Explain an important disagreement through its conditions or leave its cause unresolved.
Use a table only when the reader benefits from comparing the same attributes across cases.
Explain what the comparison implies in prose.

## Carry the reasoning across gaps

Introduce a concept when the next inference depends on it.
Use the same term for the same concept throughout the account.
For a proposed causal chain, state its links and the support for each link.
Keep correlation, mechanism hypotheses, and demonstrated causal effects distinct.
State a necessary assumption beside the conclusion that depends on it.
Give a material alternative its strongest available support before explaining the current judgment.
Describe the evidence that would favor that alternative or weaken the current answer.
Retain a genuine disagreement when the available evidence cannot select one explanation.

## Keep support local and honest

Place a source location beside the proposition it supports.
Use a page, section, figure, table, or exact artifact location when available.
Preserve distinctions between what a source reports and what the report's author infers from it.
Name the actual inspection level when it limits the synthesis: abstract, selected passages, full text, or inspected data and code.
Do not infer that cited methods, appendices, or implementations were inspected.
Do not infer novelty, causation, robustness, or practical value from a plausible narrative alone.
Show where supplied evidence is insufficient for the requested answer.
Keep missing comparisons and proposed tests separate from completed work.
