# Review comprehension and preserve decisions

## Test the full reading path

Read the report without opening its machine-readable records.
Try to state the following in ordinary language:

- What question does the report answer, and why does that question matter here?
- What judgment does it reach, under which conditions, and with which limits?
- Which observations or source comparisons support that judgment?
- Which reasoning connects the evidence to the judgment?
- Which alternative or missing evidence could change it?
- Where can the reader inspect the relevant original evidence?
- What decision remains for the researcher, and what would follow from each choice?

Treat a failed answer as a specific revision target.
Add a missing explanation or navigation cue when that is within the writing scope.
Expose a missing scientific premise as an unresolved question rather than supplying a plausible answer.
Check the summary only after the full account passes this reading check.
Remove summary claims that the full account cannot support.
Retain the full account's qualifications even when they make the summary less decisive.

## Make the review actionable

Locate each material question at a named section, paragraph, comparison, or figure.
State the exact judgment or proposed action under review.
Offer concrete options that the evidence and current authority allow.
Explain what each option changes in the claim, interpretation, further work, or scope.
Include the evidence location needed to assess those consequences.
Distinguish a choice of wording from a choice of scientific interpretation.
Point to any existing authoritative decision record instead of creating another editable approval status.
Where no such record exists, present the question in the requested report or response.
Keep unresolved decisions visible without turning them into approval events.

## Bind views without replacing the argument

Use one authoritative scientific text for the summary, full account, and any rendered version.
Bind its exact revision and evidence versions through the project's existing conventions.
When no convention exists, use a concise source note that identifies the exact supplied artifacts and versions.
Reuse an existing claim or source ID as an inspection aid.
Give readers a meaningful sentence and evidence location rather than a bare ID.
Inspect summaries, captions, tables, and translations for changes in scientific meaning.
Check denominators, comparison conditions, uncertainty, and causal language as well as headline results.
A successful build or matching digest cannot establish that different views mean the same thing.

Preserve a presented scientific version when preparing a later scientific revision.
Follow existing review requirements before using that revision for dependent work.
A request for language or organization edits permits those edits within its stated scope.
It does not silently authorize a new explanation, stronger conclusion, changed evidence, or new experiment.
An AI critique can identify a concern and propose responses for human review.
Its technical acceptance does not authorize the scientific revisions it recommends.
Apply any actual human approval or scoped waiver according to its existing limits.
When no host workflow exists, do not introduce stage gates, record schemas, or separate approval files.
