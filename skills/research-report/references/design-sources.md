# Design sources and scope

Read this file when assessing the skill's design rationale.
These are writing guides, not empirical validation of this skill.
They supply no authority to change scientific claims or research permission.

## Sources inspected on 2026-10-27

[UNC Writing Center: Literature Reviews](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/literature-reviews/), sections "Find a focus", "Consider organization", and "Summarize and synthesize".
The guide explains organizing a review around connected ideas and comparing sources within that organization.
This supports the skill's question-driven comparisons rather than a sequence of isolated paper summaries.
The guide's complete webpage was read; its example papers were not independently read.
No handout text or illustration is redistributed here.

[MIT EECS Communication Lab: Abstract](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/eecs/commkit/abstract/), sections "Criteria for Success" and "Analyze Your Audience".
The guide connects audience, problem, main finding, approach, and implication in a concise abstract.
This supports a reader-specific summary with enough context to interpret the answer.
The complete webpage was read; its linked annotated example was not inspected.
No diagram, example abstract, or fixed word-count rule is copied into this skill.

## Local design choices

Summary-to-body fidelity, exact scientific-version binding, concrete review choices, and separation of expression from scientific revision come from the user's requirements and the host workflow's existing protections.
The four report types are adaptable reasoning paths, not a universal section checklist.
The unfamiliar-reader check is an observable acceptance task proposed for this trial.
Neither guide validates the LaTeX templates or proves that this candidate improves comprehension.
