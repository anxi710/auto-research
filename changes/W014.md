Status: adopted.

## Intent and authority

The workflow must support research from an initial idea through a complete paper.
Researchers must also be able to enter at any stage with existing work.
Human review is the requested default for every intermediate research output and unresolved research choice.
Only an explicit user instruction can waive review within its stated scope.
Multi-agent work must support both role assignments and assignments across the research process.

The user's original request requires separate review and approval of each improvement.
This record presents the design and the first implementation trial.
The user approved that trial with the instruction to proceed according to the proposal.
That approval retains the proposal's separate human decision before candidate adoption.
It does not authorize the later rounds.
The user then approved adoption after the candidate, evidence, and limits were presented.
That decision activates the shared human review rule in this repository's skill package.
The current conversation follows the user's request for review before dependent work proceeds.

## Baseline capabilities and gaps

The [skill entry](../skills/research-workflow/SKILL.md) already routes work to stages S0 through S7.
The [stage guide](../skills/research-workflow/references/workflow.md) permits selective entry, iteration, writing, and review.
Standalone tasks already avoid empty records for unrelated stages.
These capabilities provide a base for independent modules.

In version `0.1.6`, the skill treated completion conditions as evidence tests rather than permission requests.
The collaboration procedure allowed an AI coordinator or delegated reviewer to accept a result.
Neither rule provided the requested default human decision before dependent research continued.
The templates also lacked a shared record of pending human review and scoped review waivers.

Existing ideas need an explicit entry path that preserves the researcher's original proposal.
Existing results need an entry path that permits missing original plans without inventing a retrospective protocol.
Writing already covers sections, figures, citations, and revisions.
Its outputs need separate review points for the outline, draft sections, figures, and complete manuscript.
The existing tests do not establish a complete research-to-paper cycle.

## Proposed human review rule

A review waiver is explicit permission to omit specified human reviews.
A research output is material that communicates evidence, a proposal, a result, or a scientific judgment.
Examples include source notes, candidate questions, plans, analysis, figures, manuscript drafts, and AI review reports.
Subagent outputs follow the same rule.

The assistant prepares the currently requested output within the approved scope.
It presents that output, its evidence, uncertainties, alternatives, and the decision needed from the researcher.
It records the output version and marks human review as pending.
It waits before using that output to advance dependent research.
Technical inspection can prepare the review without authorizing a dependent research task.
Internal file operations do not become separate research deliverables.

Several outputs can appear in one review package if each item is identified and remains separately reviewable.
Approval applies only to the presented versions and stated next actions.
Silence, elapsed time, task completion, a tool permission, or a successful test does not establish approval.
A clear approval in the current context remains valid without repeated questions.
Changed outputs return to review before dependent work uses them.
Rejection preserves the rejected version and records the requested revision.

The researcher decides goals, interpretations to investigate, priorities, budgets, and whether to revise, continue, or stop.
The assistant presents every unresolved research choice instead of silently choosing an assumption.
It asks the researcher for missing context, available evidence, or a decision about how to proceed.
Missing scientific evidence remains unknown after a human decision.
Approval can authorize investigation, narrow a claim, or stop work.
It cannot establish novelty, causation, or an experimental result without evidence.

A waiver records its source, covered outputs or stages, limits, and end condition.
The assistant applies it only within that scope.
Review resumes when the waiver ends or the researcher revokes it.
A broad instruction to continue does not silently waive review.
A waiver does not extend the experiment budget or authorize publication.
An unclear waiver requires clarification before dependent work proceeds.

AI assessment, execution completion, and human approval remain separate facts.
An AI review can recommend acceptance or revision.
The researcher decides whether the project advances unless a valid waiver applies.
The existing record checker remains a structural check and cannot prove human authorization.
Old AI acceptance records remain unchanged and do not become human approval records.

## Proposed module interface

Each module defines its entry materials, necessary inputs, intended output, evidence conditions, human decisions, and permitted next actions.
It records missing inputs and the limits of the available evidence.
It can finish a standalone task or update an existing project.
It does not require unrelated earlier stages or create empty records for them.
Shared review rules stay in one procedure rather than separate copies in every module.

| Module | Direct entry examples | Reviewable outputs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope | A topic, practical problem, or project constraint. | Goal, limits, success criteria, and resource plan. |
| Study | A question, seed paper, or existing collection. | Source notes, evidence map, and open issues. |
| Question | An existing idea, observation, or failed attempt. | Refined questions, competing explanations, and testable claims. |
| Design | A selected question or a flawed existing plan. | Comparisons, measures, controls, stopping rules, and budget. |
| Run | An approved experiment plan and usable resources. | Implementation evidence, run records, and actual outputs. |
| Analyze | New outputs or results imported from another project. | Analysis, figures, supported claims, and limits. |
| Write | An approved evidence set, outline, or existing manuscript. | Outline, sections, figures, references, and complete manuscript. |
| Review | Any research output or external review comments. | Criticism, unresolved disagreements, and proposed revisions. |

For an existing idea, preserve the original before proposing revisions.
Ask only for inputs required by the requested module.
Offer another module when it resolves a missing prerequisite, and let the researcher choose that change in scope.
For imported results, record source, available code, data, run context, and missing history.
Label new analyses as exploratory when no prior analysis plan exists.
Do not reconstruct a plan as if it preceded the results.

The researcher can return from analysis to design or from writing to evidence gathering.
That return identifies which approved outputs are affected and which require another review.
Completing a manuscript does not authorize submission.
A complete cycle requires a separate real research task and researcher participation.

## Proposed collaboration design

The coordinator maintains shared records and presents decisions to the researcher.
Each task identifies both its research purpose and the role needed to perform it.
Use separate contexts when an independent assessment is useful and supported by the harness.
Record the limitation when one assistant performs several roles.
Do not create agents for every stage by default.

| Assignment type | Example | Human decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roles within a stage | A designer proposes a comparison. A reviewer inspects validity and feasibility. | Approve or revise the plan. |
| Tasks across a process | One worker finds candidate problems. Another refines an approved question. Another tests its assumptions. | Review each output before dependent work. |
| Competing investigations | Separate workers inspect alternative explanations under one shared budget. | Choose the next test while preserving disagreement. |

Independent tasks can run together within the approved scope and total budget.
Dependent tasks wait for the relevant human approval or waiver.
The coordinator presents disagreements with their evidence instead of treating an agent vote as a scientific conclusion.
The current contract, immutable result, and review records remain the basis for traceability.
This design does not require a new scheduler or new task JSON format.

| Case | Required candidate behavior |
| --- | --- |
| An idea produces a refined question without a waiver. | Present the version for human review before designing a dependent experiment. |
| An AI reviewer accepts an analysis. | Keep human approval pending and withhold dependent writing. |
| A researcher approves a presented version and next action. | Proceed within that approval without asking the same question again. |
| A researcher rejects an output or its approved version changes. | Preserve history and seek review for the revised version. |
| A waiver covers one literature batch. | Work only within its limits and restore review when that scope ends. |
| The researcher revokes an active waiver. | Restore review before further dependent work and preserve the prior authorization history. |
| Research choices or empirical facts remain unresolved. | Ask for missing context, available evidence, or the next decision. Preserve unsupported facts as unknown. |
| A session resumes with pending review. | Restore the pending decision and do not infer approval from task completion. |
| A bulk request lacks a waiver for dependent intermediate outputs. | Use reviewed steps instead of the uninterrupted runner. |

Retain standalone task behavior, negative results, unknown job states, immutable attempts, and current record formats.

Record actual outputs, failures, review limits, and changed files.
